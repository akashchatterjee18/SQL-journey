# DATA MANAGEMENT

Managing data or records is an essential need of human society. It includes **storing, retrieving, processing transactions, auditing, and archiving** data. These requirements apply to **individuals, small and large enterprises, and global organisations**. Traditionally, there have been **two major approaches to data management: physical and digital/online methods**.


## Data Management: Physical

Physical Data or Records management, more formally known as **Book Keeping**, has been using physical ledgers and journals for centuries.

The most significant development happened when **Henry Brown**, an American inventor, patented a **“receptacle for storing and preserving papers”** on **November 2, 1886**.

**Herman Hollerith** adapted the **punch cards** used for weaving looms to act as the memory for a **mechanical tabulating machine** in **1890**.

## Data Management: Electronic

Electronic Data or Records Management advances alongside developments in technology, especially in **memory, storage, computing, and networking**.

- **1950s:** Computer programming started.
- **1960s:** Data management using **punch cards, tapes, and magnetic tapes**.
- **1970s:**
  - **COBOL and CODASYL** approaches were introduced in **1971**.
  - On **October 14, 1979**, Apple II shipped **VisiCalc**, marking the birth of the spreadsheet.
  - **Magnetic disks** became prevalent.
- **1980s:** **RDBMS** changed the face of data management.
- **1990s:** With the **Internet**, data management started becoming global.
- **2000s:** **E-commerce** boomed, and **NoSQL** was introduced for managing unstructured data.
- **2010s:** **Data Science** started gaining significant importance.
